# Endfield Efficient Exploration: Chest Routes & Stamina

Endfield's open world is visitable, but "is it worth visiting" works differently than in most open worlds. A community consensus: **chest rewards are low relative to effort** — dozens of chests can equal a single pull. Add repetitive puzzles, common invisible walls, and limited mobility, and blind full-map chest hunting is a poor trade. This guide helps you spend stamina where it pays.

## 1. Why chests feel "unworth it"

Endfield chests aren't useless — the reward per unit of time is just low. Experience says the resources from opening dozens of chests land around the magnitude of a single pull. Considering chests need running, small puzzles, and invisible-wall detours, the time cost far exceeds that payoff.

The conclusion isn't "don't open chests," but "don't make chest hunting your main resource source." Treat it as grab-on-the-way, not daily homework.

## 2. Three real exploration pains

1. **Repetitive environmental puzzles**: many are variants of the same "button-press sequence," fatigue sets in fast, novelty stays low.
2. **Common invisible walls**: the map looks passable but air-walls block you, wasting runs and breeding frustration.
3. **Limited mobility**: no climbing, no gliding — only basic jumping and **ziplines**. Vertical space and far points depend on the existing zipline network, capping freedom and run efficiency.

Stacked together, these push the value of "carpet exploration" even lower.

## 3. Priority: story and dailies before chests

A steadier resource rhythm:

- **Push the main story first**: plot and stage progression give fixed rewards well above scattered chests, and unlock later systems.
- **Then dailies**: daily missions give stable currency (e.g. ~200 Oroberyl/day), a sustainable pull source — don't skip them for chest hunting.
- **Grab chests on the way**: open ones you pass during tasks; don't detour to clear the map.

For currency sources and daily output, see the [Endfield Currency Guide](/en/endfield/currencies).

## 4. Where stamina pays best

Endfield stamina should go to **high-return, repeatable** activities, not low-density chest hunting:

1. **Daily missions / commissions**: stable, low-cost, top priority.
2. **Repeatable resource nodes**: materials that feed factory lines (batteries, capsules, Sandleaf, etc.) pay off long-term better than one-time chests.
3. **Outpost orders**: passive Conference Vouchers, compounding with dailies.
4. **Chests**: only as on-the-way behavior, no separate stamina budget.

Simple formula: **stamina → dailies + line materials > one-time chests**.

## 5. Small chest-route tips

If you still want to clear a region's chests, lower the cost:

- Plan routes along the **zipline network**, chaining chests with existing mobility, cutting wall-detour walks.
- Open "on-task" chests first; save the rest for when better movement exists.
- Treat puzzle chests as easter eggs; don't run far just for a button sequence.

## 6. FAQ

**Q: Should I clear every chest?**
A: Not as a goal. Dozens of chests roughly equal one pull; full clears cost too much time. Grab on the way.

**Q: Why does running the map feel awkward?**
A: Many invisible walls, no climbing or gliding, only basic jump and ziplines — mobility freedom is inherently limited, so run efficiency is low by design.

**Q: Where should stamina go first?**
A: Dailies and repeatable resource nodes (feeding factory lines) first; chests last, as a side behavior.

**Q: Are puzzles worth doing?**
A: Mostly repetitive button sequences with low novelty; unless the reward is a material you lack, don't invest dedicated time. For overall pace see the [Endfield Beginner Guide](/en/endfield/beginner).
