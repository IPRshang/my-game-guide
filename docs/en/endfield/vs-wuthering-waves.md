# Endfield vs Wuthering Waves: Which Urban Gacha to Play

Arknights: Endfield is constantly compared with Wuthering Waves — both are urban/open-world gacha action games with heavily overlapping audiences. But Endfield has its own clear strengths and gaps: it is praised for story, visuals, mobile optimization, and a unique factory system; it is criticized for slow combat, limited exploration freedom, and **lacking a daily login hook**. Below, five axes help you decide which to pick.

## 1. Combat feel: WuWa is snappier, Endfield is slower

Wuthering Waves builds its reputation on fluid combos and responsive controls — feel is its biggest word-of-mouth edge. Endfield is widely rated "slow and grindy": the auto-attack loop banks skill points only on specific hits, bursts are gated behind that banking rhythm, and fights drag; turret stages are a bit faster, turret-less stages drag more.

**Pick**: want snappy combos → Wuthering Waves. Can accept a strategic, management-leaning pace → Endfield.

## 2. Gacha fairness: Endfield's char pity is friendlier, weapon banner weaker

Endfield's base pity is 120 pulls for the featured character; duplicate pity climbs to 240 for the second copy; maxing needs about 5 copies, fewer than the 6 common in HoYoverse titles. But note: **pity does NOT carry over between banners** — switching resets it — and the **weapon banner rates are unfavorable**, so pulling weapons needs caution. The preview gave roughly 300 free pulls, barely enough to max one character.

Wuthering Waves runs a different weapon/character cadence, generally "character-banner friendly."

**Pick**: bothered by max-cost → Endfield's character side is friendlier. But fear the weapon-banner trap and often skip banners → weigh the no-carryover cost. Details in the [Endfield Gacha & Pity Guide](/en/endfield/gacha-pity).

## 3. Exploration freedom: WuWa freer, Endfield limited

Endfield's open world has hard constraints: many chests pay low, environmental puzzles are mostly repetitive button sequences, invisible walls are common, and there is **no climbing or gliding** — only basic jumping and ziplines. That caps vertical exploration and point-to-point efficiency.

Wuthering Waves is usually smoother on map mobility and exploration density.

**Pick**: "roam the map" as core fun → Wuthering Waves. Endfield exploration fits "grab on the way," not "deep dive."

## 4. Factory system: Endfield's unique edge

This is where Endfield differs from Wuthering Waves and most gacha games. Its AIC automated complex produces offline, and endgame recipes like Sandleaf are deeply tied to production planning — a system with real management joy (see the [Endfield Factory Guide](/en/endfield/factory-guide)). Wuthering Waves has no equivalent loop.

**Pick**: want "gacha + automation factory" → Endfield is almost alone. Pure action fans don't care about this axis.

## 5. Daily hook: Endfield is seen as missing one

Players praise Endfield's story, art, character models, and English voice acting; mobile optimization is solid (consistent 60 FPS). The criticism centers on one point: **no daily login hook** — weak stickiness for a daily check-in; plus lengthy tutorials, and Version 1.2 left players with little to do for weeks (a "dry period").

Wuthering Waves keeps a denser daily/event rhythm and stronger return motive.

**Pick**: want "something to do every day" stickiness → Wuthering Waves is steadier. Can accept burst grinding with self-found fun during dry spells → Endfield's content quality still holds.

## 6. One-line summary & FAQ

**Summary**: want feel and daily stickiness → Wuthering Waves. Want story, visuals, mobile optimization, and the one-of-a-kind factory loop, tolerating slow pace and no daily hook → Endfield. They aren't mutually exclusive; choose by the axis you care about most.

**Q: Both are urban open-world gachas — what's the core difference?**
A: Endfield adds an automation factory system and is praised for story/visuals/mobile optimization; Wuthering Waves wins on combat feel and a stickier daily rhythm.

**Q: Is Endfield's gacha cheaper than Wuthering Waves?**
A: Character side maxes at ~5 copies (better than 6), but pity doesn't carry over and the weapon banner is unfavorable, so skipping banners costs more. Currency details in the [Endfield Currency Guide](/en/endfield/currencies).

**Q: Is Endfield exploration bad?**
A: Not bad, just low value and freedom — low chest pay, repetitive puzzles, no climbing/gliding. Good for grab-on-the-way, poor for deep diving.
