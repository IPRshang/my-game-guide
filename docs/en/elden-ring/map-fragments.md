# Elden Ring Map Fragments Guide

**Elden Ring hides its map behind fragments. Each region has a map stele — a small stone pillar usually sitting at a crossroads — and interacting with it reveals that region's map. Until you find it, the area stays blank on your map screen.**

## How to recognise a map stele

- Look for a **short stone pillar with a glowing top**, often beside a road or at a junction.
- **Sites of Grace** nearby often point toward it.
- On the map screen, an **unexplored region shows a faint marker** where the stele sits once you are close enough.

## Practical approach

1. **Follow roads first** — steles are almost always near main paths, not deep in wilderness.
2. **Ride along the region's main route** on Torrent before exploring side paths.
3. **Grab the fragment as soon as you enter a new region** — exploring blind is much harder.
4. **Use the revealed map to plan** — it marks roads, ruins and landmarks that guide your next moves.

## Why fragments matter

- An unrevealed map makes it easy to miss whole dungeons and bosses.
- Revealing a region clarifies the **natural progression route**.
- Large regions may have **more than one fragment**, covering separate halves.

## Tips

- If your map is blank and you feel lost, you probably missed a stele — backtrack to the main road.
- You do not need a fragment to enter or clear an area; it only affects visibility.
- Mark points of interest manually once the map is revealed to save time later.

## FAQ

**Q: Do I need map fragments to progress the story?**
A: No — they are a convenience. The story is completable without revealing every map.

**Q: Can I miss a map fragment permanently?**
A: No. All regions remain accessible in free roam, so you can always return for one you missed.

**Q: Why is part of my map still dark after finding the fragment?**
A: The fragment reveals the terrain; you still need to physically reach areas to fill in detail and discover sites of grace.
