# NTE AI Asset Controversy: The Doubao Watermark Incident

After the August 2026 Version 1.3 update, Neverness to Everness (NTE) found itself back in the spotlight over "AI assets." A seahorse prop inside the game was found to carry a watermark from **Doubao** — a free AI chatbot by ByteDance whose image-generation feature stamps its output with a signature watermark. The presence of that watermark strongly suggested the asset had been generated or processed with an AI image tool.

## Timeline of the incident

After 1.3 went live, players sharing in-game screenshots noticed that a seahorse-shaped object bore a clearly visible "Doubao" watermark. Because that watermark is a hallmark of Doubao's image-generation function, the community quickly read it as evidence that NTE had used AI image tools in its art assets.

The discovery revived an earlier debate. Even before 1.3, the community had questioned whether NTE used AI assets; the watermark turned that suspicion from speculation into what many treated as proof, and the discussion spread rapidly across social platforms.

## The official position

In response, Hotta Studio issued a statement. The studio said that **AI-assisted tools were used only on a small number of background and environmental assets**, and emphasized that **characters and story were not created with AI**.

The key points of that statement:
- The use was limited to a "small number" of background / environmental assets.
- Characters and narrative content were not produced by AI.

## Community reaction

Despite the explanation, the community remained critical of the studio's quality control. The core frustration was less about "whether AI was used" and more about two things:

1. **The leaked watermark** implied a gap in the QA pipeline — had AI assets gone through proper review, the watermark should never have reached the live build.
2. **Trust cost**: beyond the "small number of background assets" claim, players found it hard to confirm whether other assets were also AI-processed, or to independently verify the official wording.

Some players took a more measured view: given cost and production pressure, using AI assistance for background assets is common across the industry, and the real failure was the execution slip of "not removing the watermark," rather than the decision to use AI at all.

## Current status

As of now, the AI-asset controversy remains a debated, divided topic within the NTE community, with no single consensus. It is at once a question about production process and a reflection of players' expectation that content be hand-crafted.
