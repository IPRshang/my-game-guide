# NTE Beginner Guide

New to Neverness to Everness? Here is the efficient first-day path.

Most "first-day" advice for gacha ARPGs tells you to pull immediately. In NTE that's backwards: the tutorial unlocks your code entry and banners, and the single biggest day-one advantage is **free materials from codes plus a planned first pull** — not raw progression speed. Read the [NTE codes index](/en/nte/codes/) before you spend a single currency, then commit.

> **Beginner trap we see constantly:** players pull on the first banner they see, then regret it when the limited character they wanted rotates in. NTE's currency is scarce early — treat your first target as a decision, not an impulse.

## First-day checklist

1. **Finish the tutorial** to unlock banners and code redemption.
2. **Redeem codes** from the [codes page](./codes.md) — free materials matter early.
3. **Explore the starter district** to learn traversal and combat feel.
4. **Pull your first target** using the [tier list](./tier-list.md) as a guide.

**Our take:** step 2 is the one most players undervalue. A starter pack's materials are worth more on day one than at any other point, because they accelerate your core team before the first difficulty wall.

## Resource priorities

- **Currency for banners** — bank it; don't spend on every banner.
- **Upgrade materials** — focus your core team, not the whole roster.
- **Exploration tokens** — they unlock map rewards and progression.

**Our take:** the instinct to spread upgrade materials across the roster feels safe but slows you. NTE's early game is won by a small, well-built core — pick 1–2 characters and pour into them.

## Tips

- Use the [Gacha Simulator](./gacha.md) to plan pity before spending.
- Don't over-level early — progression gates matter more than raw level.
- Follow official Discord for code drops and event timing.

**Our take:** the gacha simulator isn't just for fun — it tells you how much currency a *realistic* pity run costs, which is the difference between "I might get them" and "I will get them." Plan before you pull.

## Common mistakes

- Pulling without a plan (wasted currency).
- Ignoring exploration rewards.
- Skipping mail / codes.

## Common Mistakes — Our Analysis

The bullets above are the surface-level errors; here's the *why* behind them, based on how NTE's early economy actually works:

1. **Pulling without a plan.** NTE hands out limited launch currency, and banners rotate. An unplanned pull isn't just "wasted currency" — it's a character slot you can't get back when the one you wanted arrives. Fix: decide your first target from the [NTE hub](/en/nte/README/) before opening any banner.
2. **Ignoring exploration rewards.** Exploration tokens gate map progression; skip them and you stall exactly where the game expects you to be stronger. Fix: clear starter-district tokens before pushing story.
3. **Skipping mail / codes.** Mail and codes are the only *free*, time-limited value in the loop. Fix: redeem on arrival and check the codes page each version.

## FAQ

**Q: What should I do on my first day in NTE?**
A: Finish the tutorial to unlock banners and code redemption, redeem codes, explore the starter district, and pull your first target using the tier list.

**Q: Should I level every character I pull?**
A: No — focus upgrade materials on a small core team. NTE's early difficulty is tuned around a few built characters, not a wide bench, so depth beats breadth in the first weeks.

**Q: How do I avoid wasting my first pull?**
A: Use the [gacha simulator](./gacha.md) to understand pity cost, then pick a single target from the tier list and bank currency until you can guarantee them rather than gambling on the launch banner.
