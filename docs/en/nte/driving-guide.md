# NTE Vehicle Driving Tips: Handling the Clunky Feel

As an urban-anomaly open world, Neverness to Everness (NTE) makes vehicles a big part of exploring the city. Yet many players report that NTE's **vehicle handling feels clunky and underdeveloped** — steering and acceleration/deceleration lack smooth feedback, making it easy to lose control or hit walls at speed.

The tips below can't "fix" the underlying physics (that needs an official pass), but they can **lower your frustration and help you drive more steadily**.

## 1. Speed control comes first

The clunky feel is worst at high speed. The most direct fix is to **slow down on purpose**:

- In city streets and dense corners, keep to low/mid speed to leave room for steering.
- Speed up only on straightaways; don't discover too late that you can't brake in a corner.
- Make "anticipatory braking" a habit: ease off the throttle the moment you see an intersection, pedestrian, or obstacle.

## 2. Pick the right vehicle

Handling differs a lot between vehicles. Suggestions:

- Beginners should favor **small, nimble cars** with higher steering tolerance.
- Big / high-performance cars are faster but amplify the clunky feel — switch later once comfortable.
- Try several and keep the one you personally control best; don't blindly chase top speed.

## 3. Plan a practice route

Feel is trained, not given. Use a **low-traffic route with moderate (not extreme) corners** and run it repeatedly:

- First, pure steering: low-speed corners to learn the steering deadzone and self-centering.
- Then add a little speed: practice "brake before corner — hold through it — accelerate out."
- Finally combine on a busy main road.

Treat practice as learning the system, not racing, and your mindset stays relaxed.

## 4. Beyond driving

- If you crash or stutter frequently while driving, that worsens the feel — steady performance first via the [NTE Crash Fix Guide](/en/nte/fix-crash).
- For stamina and daily resource planning, see the [NTE Beginner Guide](/en/nte/beginner).

## FAQ

**Q: NTE driving feels clunky — what do I do?**
A: Deliberately slow down, pick a nimble small car, and practice on a low-traffic route; that's the most direct relief.

**Q: What car should a beginner pick?**
A: Favor a small, agile car with higher steering tolerance; big cars exaggerate the clunky feel.

**Q: I keep hitting walls / losing control?**
A: Usually too much speed plus poor anticipation; build the habit of "slow down before the corner" and it improves markedly.
